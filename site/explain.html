How it works

The Chance 90min figures and how xPoints is calculated

The idea

A player's xPoints for a gameweek comes from two steps:

  1. If he plays the full 90 minutes, how many points he is expected to get from each part of the FPL scoring (goals, assists, clean sheet, bonus and so on). This is what the Chance 90min page shows.
  2. Scale by his expected minutes (xMin, on the Minutes page). Parts that build up with time on the pitch are multiplied by xMin/90. Clean sheet and goals conceded only count with 60+ minutes, so they are multiplied by his chance of playing 60+ minutes.

xPoints = appearance points + Σ (points from each part over 90 minutes × minutes factor)

In a double gameweek each match is worked out on its own and the two are added up.

Step 1: minutes (xMin)

  • The 5-4-3-2-1 formula: take his last 5 matches, weight the latest by 5, the one before by 4, then 3, 2 and 1, and divide by 15. For example, latest first 90, 90, 60, 90, 0 gives xMin = (90×5 + 90×4 + 60×3 + 90×2 + 0×1) / 15 = 78.
  • Matches he missed for reasons other than selection are skipped: yellow and red card bans, the match he was sent off in, a tactical sub after a team-mate's red card (a keeper, defender or central midfielder sent off before the 70th minute, and the club's first change within 20 minutes takes him, an attacking player, off for a defensive one), injuries (from the Injuries log and any the admin added), the match he was taken off injured in (from FotMob's match events), recovery matches back from an injury in which he played less than his usual minutes from before it (the first match back, or up to 14 or 28 days after it by how long he was out), a goalkeeper's part match coming on for an injured team-mate, and the match he got hurt in when FPL flags him Doubtful. A match he was taken off injured in, sent off in or taken off as a tactical sub, or a recovery match, still counts if he played at least the usual minutes the formula gives without it, since keeping it can only raise them. Older matches fill the gap so there are always 5. A match he plays while flagged (say a 25% flag, but he starts anyway) counts as usual, and so does any match he plays during a logged injury.
  • Red cards: he misses the ban, then goes back to 100% of the xMin he had before the card.
  • FPL flags: a 75% flag multiplies next gameweek's xMin by 75%. Doubtful only affects the next match. An injury with a return date gives 0 until that gameweek. One without a return date is out for the typical number of days for that injury, counted from the date of the FPL news (the median days out of about 1,900 injuries of Premier League players in 2024-25 and 2025-26 on Soccerway: a knock, illness or unspecified injury 7 days, a head or shoulder injury 10, hip 11, back 14, groin 16, muscle, thigh, calf or ankle 21, knee 24, foot 33, hamstring 42, a fracture 56, surgery 70, ACL or Achilles 270), never back for the next gameweek. Back from an injury (with a date or without), his minutes follow how long he was out, counted in days so an international break in between counts as recovery: under 2 weeks out, 80% of his usual minutes in the first match back, then full; 2 to 4 weeks, 55% in the first and 90% for the next 14 days; 4 weeks or more, 40% in the first, 70% for 14 days and 85% to day 28; then full (from about 480 returns of regular starters in 2024-25 and 2025-26).
  • Goalkeepers: only one plays, so when a first-choice goalkeeper is out or flagged and due back, his stand-in's minutes drop by the starter's share of each match.
  • Admin min: a figure the admin sets on the Admin page replaces the formula for everyone. On the Minutes page, any viewer can also set their own xMin; those edits stay in their browser only.
  • Start: the same 5-4-3-2-1 weights over the share of matches he started, times the share of his usual minutes he is expected to play.
  • Appearance 1+ and 60+: the same 5-4-3-2-1 weights over the share of matches he played at all and the share where he played 60+ minutes, multiplied by the FPL flag.

Step 2: the Chance 90min columns

Every column except the two Appearance ones assumes he plays the full 90 minutes. A column shown as a percentage is the chance of it happening at least once; the others are expected numbers (averages). The two are linked: chance of at least one = 1 − e−expected number. For example, 0.70 expected goals in total gives 1 − e−0.70 ≈ 50% to score at least once.

Every rate blends two things: the player's own record (recent matches count more; a match's weight halves every 12 gameweeks) and the average of players in the same role. The fewer minutes he has, the more he is pulled towards that average, so one lucky match does not inflate his figures.

The full xPoints formula

For one match, with m = xMin/90, P1 = Appearance 1+ and P60 = Appearance 60+:

PartFormula
AppearanceP1 + P60
Goals(NPxGoals + Pen goals) × m × goal points for his position (FWD 4, MID 5, DEF 6, GK 10).
Both figures are per 90 minutes on the pitch, so both are multiplied by m once: he can only score, or take a penalty, while he is on. Pen goals = team penalties per match × his share of them while he is on × 0.79. The Pen share on the Set pieces page already includes m (his share of all the team's penalties in the match), so it is not multiplied by m again: for a number 1 taker with xMin 85 and 0.25 team penalties a match, 0.25 × 100% × 0.79 × 85/90 = 0.25 × 94% × 0.79 = 0.19 penalty goals.

m60 = his minutes in a match where he plays 60+: the same last 5 matches and 5-4-3-2-1 weights as xMin, counting only the matches where he played 60+ (for example 90, 68, 90, a cameo, 90 → (90×5 + 68×4 + 90×3 + 90×1) / (5+4+3+1) = 83). Only goals while he is on the pitch count, so a player taken off at 70 keeps more clean sheets than one who plays 90.

When you edit xMin on the Minutes or xPoints page, the site recalculates with this formula at the new minutes. Appearance points then follow FPL rules: 1 point if the minutes are above 0, 1 more at 60 or above.
